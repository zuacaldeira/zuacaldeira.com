Step 1

Frame: where the languages go

Recommended

A · In the bar

For: one tap to German from anywhere, on a phone too, with no extra height.

Costs: codes instead of names, and your name in the header gives way to the mark on phones and tablets. The name stays in the first line of text.

 

B · Strip above

For: "Deutsch" needs no decoding, and the name stays in the header at every width.

Costs: 45 px of the first screen on every visit, for something most English readers never use. It scrolls away.

 

C · Menu

For: the quietest header.

Costs: on a phone, German is two taps away behind the menu. The footer list is the only visible sign of it on the page.

Why A: a recruiter skimming for a minute must see German at once, and only A shows it at every scroll position on a phone.

Step 2

Hero: what sits behind the sentence

 

A · Plain

For: v2 as drawn, the calmest and fastest.

Costs: the azulejo idea is carried only by the small lattice. On cream that worked; on these glazes it looks more generic.

Recommended

B · On the tile

For: the study's lozenge grout behind v2's hero, so the tile and the Hasse diagram are the same drawing, in every glaze.

Costs: a faint pattern behind running text, though every glaze still passes contrast.

 

C · The wall

For: keeps the 74 real lattices and their credit, the most striking picture of the research.

Costs: loses the 2³ lattice and its links, and the wall speaks to researchers, while software is meant to lead.

Why B: it keeps v2's message and links and gets back the identity v2 relied on cream to carry. If you pick A or B, the wall leaves the home page; it could move to /research, and HOME-HERO-09 goes with it.

Step 3

Theorem band: how much of the paper

 

A · Theorem alone

For: v2 as drawn, one result and its evidence.

Costs: Definition 6, Lemma 8 and Proposition 18 leave the home page, so HOME-PAPER-02 to 04 need rewriting.

 

B · All four

For: keeps the agreed cases as they are, and a reviewer sees the definitions at once.

Costs: a long, dense band in the middle of a page that sells software; about a phone screen more.

Recommended

C · Unfold

For: v2's short band, with the three statements one tap away and still in the page's HTML.

Costs: a control to build and test, and the statements are hidden at first.

Why C: software leads, and a researcher still gets the definitions without leaving the page.

Where this differs from the v2 boards

  • Theorem wording: the agreed quotation, not v2's "its state space…". Quotations stay as they are.
  • Band in the dark glazes: a raised dark surface, not the lifted accent, which made a glaring slab at night.
  • Lattice links: each has a target of at least 44 px, and the labels stay readable over the edges.
  • Header: stays sticky, as agreed, and gains the languages and the three-state theme button.
  • Email: the site's hello@zuacaldeira.com, not the Gmail address on the boards.
