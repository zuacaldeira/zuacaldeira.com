Page frame: where the languages go

The frame's visible decision is how a reader changes language, because today the page has no link for it. The three options below differ only in that. Everything else in the frame is the same in all three and is listed further down. The hero and the paper statements are shown as they are; they get their own steps next. Everything is clickable: the language links, the menu on the phone, the theme button.

Recommended

A · In the bar

What it does
EN, DE and PT sit in the sticky header at every width, the current one underlined.
Optimises for
One tap to German from anywhere on the page. A recruiter on a phone sees it without looking for it.
Gives up
Codes instead of names. And the header button has to say "Kontakt" (the nav word) instead of "Kontakt aufnehmen", or the German header does not fit at 768.

 

B · Strip above

What it does
A thin strip above the header names the three languages in full. It scrolls away; the footer lists them too.
Optimises for
Clarity: "Deutsch" needs no decoding, and the header stays exactly as it is.
Gives up
45 px of the first screen, on every visit, for something researchers reading in English never use. That pushes your name further down on a phone.

 

C · Menu

What it does
One small button in the header opens the list. On a phone it moves into the drawer. The footer lists them too.
Optimises for
The quietest header, which favours the researcher who never switches.
Gives up
Discovery. On a phone, German is two taps away behind the burger menu, and nothing on the first screen says the site has German at all.

Why A

Researchers come first and read in English, so the switch must not take space from them; A adds no height. Employers skim for a minute and may read German, so it must be seen at once; A is the only option visible at every scroll position and on a phone.

The same in all three

  • One column. Header, hero text, paper and footer share one left edge.
  • Ochre and gold, not teal. The buttons take the colours their CSS already intends: 4.99:1 for the header button, 13.45:1 for the hero's first, 14.23:1 for the second against cobalt, up from about 2.2:1.
  • Links keep the language. On /de/home the menu leads to /de/about, not /about.
  • 44 px targets on every header and hero control. Today they are 40.
  • The 404 page uses the wording agreed in D9, in English, at /fr/home and every other unknown path.
